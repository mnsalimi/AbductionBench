\FloatBarrier

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:future_work}
\label{sec:limitations}

All free-form answers, trace measures, and step-relevance labels come from a single LLM judge (gpt-oss-120b); we manually reviewed random samples of its outputs and revised its prompts accordingly, but did not measure agreement with human annotators systematically. Each dataset contributes 50 records: the $\pm$ in Table~\ref{tab:main_results_all_models} reflects variation across the three samples per record, not across records, and a 95\% interval over records is roughly $\pm$7--8 points for a static score and up to $\pm$13 for an episode score, so small differences between models should not be over-read. Because of their token cost, passive and interactive episodes were run once, under IO only, and interactive results depend on the simulated patient or examiner (gpt-4o-mini). Comparisons across domains, formulations, and delivery modes are observational, since the source datasets differ in content and difficulty. Only six passive and interactive datasets exist in a form we could integrate, which reflects a gap in the field and limits analysis of these settings. Finally, written traces are observable outputs, not faithful records of the computation behind an answer. Future work should validate the judges on human-annotated samples, extend the model roster, and build controlled passive and interactive tasks that change one property at a time.
